Wake, drag and vortex shedding behind a cylinder — the mechanism behind loads and vibration on tubes, cables, chimneys and offshore risers.

Problem

A uniform stream of speed U∞ meets a circular cylinder of diameter D. Below about Re = 47 the flow is steady: two symmetric vortices sit behind the cylinder and grow longer with Re. Above it the wake becomes unstable and sheds vortices alternately from the top and bottom — the Kármán vortex street — at a frequency given by the Strouhal number St = fD/U∞.

The case tests curved walls, the wake and, at Re = 100, time accuracy. Drag, the length of the recirculation zone and the shedding frequency are compared with published data.

Why it matters for design: when the shedding frequency approaches a natural frequency of the structure, it can vibrate and fail by fatigue — a known risk for heat-exchanger tube bundles, cables, chimneys and risers. Predicting the frequency and the force is the first step of a vibration check.

Mesh

Triangles of 0.4 D in the far field, refined towards the cylinder down to 0.02 D (growth 1.04), and 14 quadrilateral prism layers on the wall: first layer 0.004 D, growth 1.15. Built with the Mesh step (a mesh definition with a refinement and an inflation on the circle).

Solver set-up

Solver
Pressure-based SIMPLE, steady, Rhie–Chow face velocities
Domain
Inlet 20 D upstream, outlet 40 D downstream, slip side walls at ±20 D (blockage 2.5 %)
Fluid
ρ = 1 kg/m³, U∞ = 1 m/s, D = 1 m, μ = 1/Re
Convection scheme
Linear upwind (second order)
Convergence
All scaled residuals below 10⁻⁶ — 1900 iterations (Re = 20), 1590 iterations (Re = 40)
Drag
Force Fx on the cylinder from Results ▸ Global Variables (pressure + viscous stresses), CD = Fx / (½ρU∞²D)

Results

At both Reynolds numbers the flow separates from the rear of the cylinder and closes into two symmetric, counter-rotating vortices; the bubble is more than twice as long at Re = 40.

Comparison with reference data

Result. The drag coefficient is within 2 % of Dennis & Chang at both Reynolds numbers and the separation angle within 1.3°. The recirculation is 7 % (Re = 20) and 15 % (Re = 40) shorter than their value — 6 % shorter than the experiment of Coutanceau & Bouard at Re = 40. The wake length is the most mesh-sensitive quantity here: the cells 1–2 D behind the cylinder are about 0.1 D, and the side walls at ±20 D (2.5 % blockage) also shorten it slightly and raise the drag. A finer wake mesh is the next step of this study.
