Problem

Fluid enters a long channel of height H with a uniform velocity Ū. Friction at the walls slows the fluid near them and speeds up the core until, after an entrance length, the profile stops changing. The fully developed flow has an exact solution, so the error of the solver can be measured, not just estimated.

Why it matters for design: pressure drop sets the pump or fan power of every piping, duct and cooling-channel system. A solver that reproduces the exact friction factor here can be trusted on more complex passages.

Results

The uniform inlet velocity is slowed by the walls; the core accelerates until, after about 2.5 H, the profile stops changing. Near the inlet the profile briefly peaks off the centreline — the known velocity overshoot of developing laminar flow at low Reynolds numbers.

Comparison with reference data

Result. In the fully developed part of the channel the pressure gradient, friction factor and wall shear stress are within 0.6 % of the exact solution, and the velocity profile within 0.5 % of the centreline speed — on an unstructured quadrilateral mesh that is not aligned with the flow.
