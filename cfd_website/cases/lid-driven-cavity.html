Lid-driven cavity

The reference test of every flow solver: a box whose lid slides and drives one large vortex. If a code fails here, no design result can be trusted.

Validated
Reynolds numbers
Re = UL/ν = 100 and 1000
Flow
Laminar, steady, incompressible
Mesh
128 × 128 quadrilaterals
Reference
Ghia et al. 1982

Problem

A square cavity of side L is filled with fluid. The top wall (the lid) slides to the right at a constant speed U; the other three walls are fixed. The moving lid drags the fluid along and sets up a large primary vortex near the centre, with small counter-rotating vortices in the bottom corners.

The geometry is trivial and there is no inlet or outlet, so the case isolates the core of the solver: pressure–velocity coupling, convection and diffusion. It is compared at Re = 100 and Re = 1000 with the multigrid results of Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982), the most widely used reference data in CFD.

Why it matters for design: recirculating flow driven by a moving wall appears in seals, bearings, mixers and coating processes. More importantly, this case shows that the pressure–velocity coupling at the heart of every simulation is right.

Geometry and boundary conditions

Boundary conditions
BoundaryType in Torvex LabCondition
Top wall (lid)Moving wallu = U, v = 0
Left, right, bottom wallsWallNo-slip: u = v = 0
Pressure levelReference cellp = 0 in one cell (closed domain)

Mesh

A uniform mesh of 128 × 128 square cells (Δx = L/128 = 0.0078 L), the same resolution as the 129 × 129 grid of Ghia et al.

Solver set-up

Solver
Pressure-based SIMPLE, steady, Rhie–Chow face velocities
Fluid
ρ = 1 kg/m³; μ = 0.01 (Re = 100) and 0.001 Pa·s (Re = 1000); U = 1 m/s, L = 1 m
Convection scheme
Linear upwind (second order)
Relaxation
u, v: 0.7 · p: 0.3
Convergence
All scaled residuals below 10⁻⁶ — 2335 iterations (Re = 100), 1686 iterations (Re = 1000)
Run time
About 5 min (Re = 100) and 4 min (Re = 1000) on one laptop core

Results

One large clockwise vortex fills the cavity; secondary counter-rotating vortices sit in the two bottom corners and grow with the Reynolds number. At Re = 1000 the primary vortex moves towards the centre and the velocity gradients near the lid steepen.

The lid velocity in post-processing

This case revealed that Results showed the velocity of the first row of cells on the lid (0.94 m/s at Re = 1000) instead of the imposed 1 m/s: values were averaged from the cell centres to the nodes. Since build 112 the nodes on a boundary take the imposed value — walls, moving walls, inlets, outlet pressure, fixed temperatures — in contours, line plots and probes.

Comparison with reference data

Result. On the 128 × 128 mesh the centreline velocities agree with Ghia et al. within 0.5 % of the lid speed for u and 0.8 % for v, at both Reynolds numbers; the primary vortex centre is within 0.005 L and its strength within 1 %.
