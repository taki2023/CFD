Lid-driven cavity

The classic first test of an incompressible solver: a square box whose lid slides, driving one large vortex.

Problem

A square cavity of side L is filled with fluid. The top wall (the lid) slides to the right at a constant speed U; the other three walls are fixed. The moving lid drags the fluid along and sets up a large primary vortex near the centre, with small counter-rotating vortices in the bottom corners.

The geometry is trivial and there is no inlet or outlet, so the case isolates the core of the solver: pressure–velocity coupling, convection and diffusion. It is compared with the multigrid results of Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982), the most widely used reference data in CFD.
